how many permutation are there in a baseball at bat sequence
The number of "permutations" in a baseball sequence depends on whether you are referring to the pitch-by-pitch count within a single at-bat or the batting order for a full game. 
1. Pitch-by-Pitch Permutations (The Count)
A single at-bat is defined by the sequence of balls and strikes. Because an at-bat must end on a specific outcome (a 3rd strike, 4th ball, or ball in play), the number of ways to reach a certain count varies: 
Total Possible Count Sequences: There are 127 distinct ways for a plate appearance to reach a conclusion based strictly on balls and strikes (excluding foul balls after two strikes, which can theoretically make a sequence infinite).
Most Common Paths:
Strikeout: There are 10 different ways to strike out (e.g., S-S-S, B-S-S-S, etc.).
Walk: There are 10 different ways to draw a walk (e.g., B-B-B-B, S-B-B-B-B, etc.).
Infinite Sequences: If you include foul balls hit with two strikes, the number of permutations is technically infinite, as a batter can foul off an unlimited number of pitches.
