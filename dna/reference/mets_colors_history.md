The New York Mets' primary colors, blue and orange, were chosen to honor the two National League teams that departed New York for California in the late 1950s: the Brooklyn Dodgers (royal blue) and the New York Giants (orange). These are also the official municipal colors of New York City, originally derived from the Dutch flag.

Color & Uniform Timeline
1962 (Inception): The original look featured Dodger blue with Giant orange trim and the Giants' interlocking "NY" cap logo. White pinstriped home jerseys also paid subtle homage to the Yankees.
The "Pink & Black" Concept: Before settling on blue and orange, the team's first majority owner, Joan Whitney Payson, considered pink and black—the racing colors of her family’s Greentree Stables.
1980s (Racing Stripes): In 1982-83, the team introduced their iconic "racing stripe" uniforms, which featured blue and orange stripes running down the shoulders and sides.
1998–2011 (The Black Era): The Mets added black as an official trim color and introduced popular black alternate jerseys and caps. These were phased out starting in 2012 to return to the "classic" look, though they were reintroduced as alternates in 2021.
2024 (City Connect): The team added purple and gray to their rotation for the City Connect series, representing the 7 Line subway train and the city's "concrete jungle".
